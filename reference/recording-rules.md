# Recording rules: how NAVI records the screen

Read this before recording anything. These rules came from building a real walkthrough end to end, including the takes that went wrong. They apply to any web app being demonstrated.

## Picture follows the voice

1. **Record to the voice; never retime footage afterwards.** Generate the voice first. Start each browser action live, at the moment its cue word is spoken in the finished take. The recording then plays at normal speed under the voice: no held frames, no speed-ups, no jumps. Retiming footage after the fact (holding a frame, then speeding up, then jumping) is what looked glitchy. *(Draft 1's rough opening; draft 2 recorded live with every action within 0.5 s of its word.)*
1b. **One live take.** The recorder is told the moment each click and hover should land (its word), and the cursor sets off its own travel time before it, so the first take is on its words (`scripts/record/record.py`, the recorder's `landAt`). A second take is recorded only when the first would fail the join check (an action more than 0.5 s from its word, usually a target that was slow to appear), with that step started earlier by what the first take measured; the better take is kept, and what was learned is kept for the next time that video is recorded (`record-leads.json`). *(Andy, 12 Nov 2026: record once by default. Before, every video was recorded two or three times to measure the cursor's travel.)*
2. **Never pause, cut or speed up the voice to fit the picture.**
3. **Show page loads at real speed and cut only after the page has settled.** A real 2 to 4 s load looks like someone using the app; a sped-up or cut-short one looks like a glitch. Put long loads at section boundaries (see `voice-rules.md`, section 6) instead of squeezing the voice. *(Draft 1 cut 0.8 s after a click.)*
4. **No still screen for more than about 5 s.** Something moves, highlights or changes at least every 4 to 5 s, and the voice names what's on screen as it happens.
5. **The first 30 to 40 s carry the most action.** Open on the app doing things, not on talk over a still. **The cursor starts moving within the first second**, so the opening is never still: when the first action would set off later, record.py starts the cursor drifting toward the middle of the page at 0.2 s. *(Andy, 10 Nov 2026: the whop video sat still for 2.2 s.)*
6. **Expect pop-ups right after a create step** (upsells, onboarding, notices). Cut on the settled page before they appear, or dismiss them on camera as part of the step.
6b. **The video ends with the voice:** about 0.7 s after the last word, on any site. A busy app (whop keeps live connections open) never "goes quiet", so the last click used to wait up to 4 s for it, leaving a 5 s still at the end; record.py now ends the take on the voice's schedule. *(Andy, 11 Nov 2026.)*
7. Join checks before any draft goes to the user: no frozen frame over 1.5 s unless the voice is on that step, no jump cuts, no cut within 1.5 s of a click.

## Look

8. **No music bed.** A walkthrough is someone talking over their screen. Faint room tone only.
9. **Nothing on screen the user didn't ask for.** No "Illustrative", "Demo" or "Example" tags, no watermark. Captions and the corner label follow the user's settings (captions burned in by default, corner label off by default). If content is made up, say so in the voice or the description.
9b. **A caption never covers what's being pointed at or clicked.** While the cursor heads for, points at or clicks something where the caption would sit, that caption moves to the top of the frame (`scripts/render/captions.py` place(), from the take's cursor log; a re-render, never a re-record). *(Andy, 10 Nov 2026: "Next, the price" sat on top of the price box.)*
10. **Recordly's macOS-style cursor (tahoe), with the page's real cursor shape** (arrow, I-beam over text boxes, hand over links). **No click ring, ripple or burst, ever:** the cursor just dips for a moment when it clicks (Recordly's click). A coloured cursor or a click animation reads as generated. *(Andy, Phase 3: the ripple "looks really ugly. It should never do that.")*
11. **Smooth eased cursor paths, and no zoom by default: the frame stays the same size the whole video.** Automatic zooms on every click read as cheap special effects in a casual demo. Zoom only when the user asks for it, or when something is too small to read, and then once, held, not in and out per click. Output at 60 fps so the cursor moves smoothly. *(Andy, Phase 3: "so many unnecessary zooms… it was just tacky to look at". See `listening/2026-11-08-phase3-recording.md`.)*
11b. **Two video styles, picked by the user:** *Casual style* (default: no zoom, no camera movement, Recordly's cursor exactly, including hiding when idle) and *Recordly style* (Recordly's own cursor, zoom and motion-blur code with its defaults, unchanged). Don't tune Recordly style, and never hand-copy its numbers: read them from Recordly's own new-project defaults (`normalizeProjectEditor({})`). Andy approved it as it is. **Recordly's files are copied in unedited.** NAVI's code only feeds them input (frames, cursor log, zoom spots). Changes go into Casual style only, and only by taking the Recordly pieces the user names.
11c. **Recordly stays on the version the user approved:** 2.0.0-beta.1, commit `b2b2f2b` (pinned in `recorder/polish/recordly/PINNED.json`). It's a beta, and a newer version could change the look. Update only when the user says so, then re-run the approved test clips and show them before switching. The build refuses to run if any Recordly file differs from the pin (`recorder/polish/check-recordly.mjs`).
11d. **Capture the browser at 2× (2880×1620 for a 1440×810 page).** Headless Chromium's screencast ignores the page's pixel ratio and sends 1× frames unless it's launched with `--force-device-scale-factor` (NAVI's recorder patch). 3× drops the capture to about 25 frames a second, which makes typing look choppy.
12. **When the user rejects an element, remove it at its source** (the script, brief or settings that asked for it), not just in the render, so the next run can't bring it back.

## Working safely in a real account

13. **The right seat, logged in by the user.** A seat is one saved login on a site, with a name: *owner* (the user's own login, which opens their businesses' dashboards). The user logs in to each seat once, from Settings > Logins; NAVI never types passwords or codes, and only ever reads the saved session (`$NAVI_DATA/logins/<site>/<seat>.json`).
13b. **Each video picks its seat from the job** (`scripts/seats/seats.py pick`): *owner on the member-facing pages* for anything a buyer sees (opening courses, modules, lessons): the course pages, never the dashboard, with no change allowed and nothing a buyer wouldn't see on screen (13e); *owner in the test business* for "how to build X" demos; *owner in a real business* only when the job names that business and needs its owner dashboard. The script carries the pick (`"seat"`) and the script preview shows it. A business is a test only when the user marked it in `$NAVI_DATA/test-businesses.json`; every other business counts as real. *(Andy, 10 Nov 2026: this replaces the old "stop and ask if the business is real" rule; the seats and the action guard do that job without stopping a batch.)*
13c. **Name anything a demo creates as a test** (since 13 Nov 2026 the cleanup also deletes NAVI's own "NAVI test" courses, with their chapters and lessons, and "NAVI test" chapters inside other courses, on the test business's own community pages (`pages` in seats.json, e.g. `whop.com/navi-test/`), with the same checks: page title, the confirm box naming the item, the guard allowing it only from the cleanup, only in a test business) ("NAVI test 1", "NAVI test 2"…), unpublished and not for sale. **In a test business only** (one the user marked in `$NAVI_DATA/test-businesses.json`), NAVI deletes the products it created itself, names starting "NAVI test", **before the first take and after every take**, so each take starts clean (`scripts/cleanup/test_products.mjs`, run by record.py). It checks the address is a marked test business, the page title names it, and the site's confirm box names the same product, and the action guard allows that delete only from the cleanup step, only in a test business. **Never delete anything in a real business.** *(Andy, 10 Nov 2026.)*
13d. **The action guard: open anything, change only what's safe** (`scripts/guard/guard.mjs` with the site's pack in `config/guard/<site>.json`; Chrome's own request interception (CDP Fetch, set on the whole browser, so the cache stays on) in every browser NAVI opens: explore, the privacy check, every recording take and the cleanup). NAVI may open and film any page, including payouts, balances, billing, bank and API key pages; the privacy blur covers every number, field and account detail on them (`reference/privacy.md`). The guard blocks only actions, in every seat: withdrawing, transfers and payouts, refunds, paying with a card, changing bank, payout or billing details, creating or revealing API keys and tokens, team or ownership changes, and deleting anything (except NAVI's own "NAVI test" products in a test business, from the cleanup). Paying is blocked on every site, including sites with no pack yet (`config/guard/_payments.json`: the addresses that pay, save a card or change a subscription at Stripe, PayPal, Braintree, Apple, Google Pay and other processors, not their background reports). In a real business it also blocks every other change unless the job explicitly asked for it, and any change it can't name (default deny). A blocked action is stopped before it leaves the browser and fails that video with the reason (`blocked.json`, `report.md`, exit code 4); NAVI never asks the user mid-run. The pack was built from logged runs on whop.com (10 Nov 2026); when a site changes, log a run again (`explore.mjs --learn`, `scripts/guard/catalogue.mjs`).
13e. **A buyer's view is filmed with the owner's login, on the member pages, as a buyer of one product.** NAVI has no second login (Andy, 11 Nov 2026). The site's own "preview as a buyer" is set before the first frame (whop: the `whop-core.preview-as-access-pass-<business>` cookie, on a copy of the login kept only for that take; the script's seat names the product as `"preview_as"`), so the page lists what that product's buyers can open. What still shows only to the owner is blurred (`config/guard/<site>.json` → `member_view.owner_only`, `scripts/privacy/member_view.js`); on whop's member pages that was: the "Preview as" switch, Dashboard and Support links, "Add app" and the "+" by a section, "Add team", "Edit social links", "Create post", the owner's other businesses in the left rail, the ⋯ menus on courses and apps (View analytics, Edit, Hide course, Delete course) and the drag handles for reordering. **Lesson pages are avoided** (Andy, 12 Nov 2026: course and module pages only; the planner, explorer and writer keep to them, and a lesson is shown by hovering it on its module page): the owner sees the course editor there (drag handles, add and delete chapter, upload, drip settings, autosave), not a buyer's lesson, so a member-view take that opens one fails with that reason. Nothing may change on member pages (the guard treats them as the owner's real account).
13f. **A video starts from its own ready state** (Andy, 13 Nov 2026). When a video needs something to exist first (a product for a course video), it is made off camera before every take, after the cleanup (`prepare.json` in the video's folder: a start page and explore-style steps; `record.py prepare`), and before the explorer's and the rehearsal's runs. A video never depends on another video's leftovers: the cleanup deletes them.
14. **Rehearse the whole flow before recording** (`scripts/record/rehearse.py`, part of `batch.py write`): every action of the script is played once, quickly, in the recorder's own browser with the script's login, before any voice is paid for. A step the recorder can't do goes back to the checks model with what the page does offer, and is rehearsed again; a script that still can't be played is skipped with that reason. *(12 Nov 2026: a script that passed every check named a link in a way the recorder couldn't find, and failed its take after the voice was made.)* Also by hand, in a disposable copy if the flow creates things. Pop-ups that only appear after a create button are what break takes.
15. **Any step that creates something (a workspace, product, business, account) needs a disposable test account or a scratch copy,** so takes can be repeated cleanly. Every retake of a create step leaves a real duplicate behind, and many apps can't delete just one. Check before recording.
16. **Don't assume a repeated name stays the same.** Apps quietly rename duplicates ("Demo Academy" became "Demo Academy 2"). Check the real name on the page before the next section.
17. **Target elements by stable attributes** (link addresses, placeholders, field values), not visible labels that change after an install.
18. **Keep every raw recording.** Look changes (cursor, captions, corner label, audio) are re-renders, never re-recordings.
19. **Never publish or sell.** Opening payouts, balances or settings is fine (rule 13d); changing them is not, unless the job is about that change and, in a real business, asks for it.

## Tools

20. **Check a recorder can be driven without a person** (command line, scripting or a local API) before choosing it. A recorder with only buttons can't go in an automated pipeline. Look in the app itself; don't guess from its website. *(One popular screen recorder had none of these.)*
21. Playwright drives the browser and records. Audio is NAVI's own takes, played as one continuous track per section; the recorder's own text-to-speech is never used.

## Never take over the Mac

22. **NAVI never takes over the user's Mac.** No bringing a window to the front, no new windows grabbing focus, no taking the mouse or keyboard. The user keeps working in other apps while NAVI explores, records and renders.
    - **Only the login is visible.** `login` opens one browser window for the user to sign in themselves; that's the only time a NAVI window is in front, and it never pulls itself back in front after the user clicks away. Its session save must not open tabs: Playwright's `context.storageState()` opens a helper tab for any site the tab already left, and in a visible browser that tab brought the window to the front every 2 seconds (whop.com login, 9 Nov 2026). The login reads cookies and each open page's storage instead.
    - **Exploring, recording, polishing and checks are always headless:** no window, no Dock icon. Explore with `scripts/explore/explore.mjs`, never a hand-written script with a visible browser; it has no option to show one.
    - **No slow-down when not in front:** Chromium runs with `--disable-background-timer-throttling`, `--disable-backgrounding-occluded-windows` and `--disable-renderer-backgrounding`.
    - **Check:** `scripts/focus/focuswatch.swift` (built to `.venv/bin/navi-focuswatch`) logs every app that comes to the front and every window NAVI's browser shows. A run passes only with none of NAVI's after the login window. The same clip recorded in front and in the background must measure the same (`scripts/focus/compare_takes.py`: frame timing, smoothness, cursor, click timing).
