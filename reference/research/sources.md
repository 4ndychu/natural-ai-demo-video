# Sources

Research pass of 5 November 2026 on how real people sound when they show or explain something on screen. Findings are in [disfluency.md](disfluency.md), [mouth-and-breath.md](mouth-and-breath.md), [unsureness.md](unsureness.md) and [casual-vs-scripted.md](casual-vs-scripted.md).

**Nothing third-party is in this repository.** Audio, transcripts and corpus files stay in `output/research/` on Andy's Mac (gitignored) and are deleted when the research is done. Only aggregate numbers and short quotes are kept here.

"Full text" = read in full; "abstract" or "secondary" = the paper's own results weren't seen, so the claim comes from its abstract or another paper's summary.

## Tools

- CrisperWhisper 2.0 (nyralabs): verbatim speech recognition that keeps fillers, repeats and cut-off words. https://huggingface.co/nyralabs/CrisperWhisper2.0_medium. Code MIT; model weights and outputs under the nyra health Non-Commercial Research License.
- yt-dlp (audio download) and ffmpeg.
- NAVI's own measuring scripts: `scripts/research/transcribe_verbatim.py` (CrisperWhisper, first N seconds of each file) and `scripts/research/measure_speech.py` (every number in casual-vs-scripted.md). The Map Task and DisfluencySpeech counts came from one-off scripts kept with the data in `output/research/`.
